COGNI-LEARN Cognitive Learning Cycle Strategy

A digital instructional tool for functional skills in Special Education, designed around Cognitive Learning Theory.

Project: Exercise #5
Jasmine Jing M. Mangayan
Subject: MSE510 Models, Theories and Instructional Strategies

Project Overview

The instructional strategy used in this project is a Cognitive Learning Cycle Strategy based on Cognitive Learning Theory. The strategy is designed to help learners actively process information instead of simply receiving it. It guides learners through a sequence of cognitive processes that support understanding, remembering, practicing, and applying skills in meaningful situations.

The strategy follows six stages:

Daily Routines & Sequencing
Following Directions
Emotions & Coping
Functional Money Skills
Functional Communication
Personal Safety & Emergency Awareness
Self-Care & Hygiene
Home & Classroom Independence
Time, Schedules & Community Skills

In addition, the system provides an AAC Support Button that can be accessed across the application to support learners who communicate through pictures, symbols, text, or other augmentative and alternative communication methods.

These lessons were selected because they focus on skills that learners may need in their everyday lives. The activities are presented through pictures, simple instructions, choices, matching tasks, sequencing activities, and real-life situations. These features are intended to make learning more active and meaningful for the learner.

Rationale of the Project

In Special Education, learners have different learning needs, abilities, and ways of responding. Some learners may need additional time, repeated practice, visual support, and clear instructions before they can use a skill independently. Because of this, instruction should not focus only on giving information. It should also provide opportunities for learners to understand, practice, and use what they have learned.

The Cognitive Interactive Learning System was developed to address this need. The lessons focus on practical skills that are useful in school, at home, in classrooms, and in familiar community settings. By practicing these skills through an interactive digital platform, learners can receive structured support while working toward greater independence.

Connection to Cognitive Learning Theory

The project is based on Cognitive Learning Theory, which gives importance to the way learners think and process information. Learning takes place when learners pay attention, understand information, connect it with previous knowledge, remember it, and use it when needed.

The activities in the system are designed to support these processes. For example, a learner completing a sequencing activity must remember the different steps of a routine and place them in the proper order. A learner following directions must read or listen to an instruction, understand what is being asked, and carry out the correct response. In an emotions activity, the learner must understand a situation, identify a possible emotion, and select an appropriate response.

The system therefore follows a simple learning process:

The strategy follows six stages:

1. Attention – The learner is introduced to clear and meaningful information through pictures, short instructions, symbols, and other visual or auditory supports.

2. Understanding – The learner identifies what the instruction or situation means and connects the new information with familiar experiences or prior knowledge.

3. Memory – The learner recalls important information, steps, choices, or relationships needed to complete the task.

4. Practice – The learner applies the information through interactive activities such as sequencing, selecting pictures, answering questions, identifying emotions, matching, and solving simple functional problems.

5. Response – The learner demonstrates understanding by choosing, arranging, communicating, or performing the appropriate response. Immediate feedback is provided to support learning and correction.

6. Application – The learner connects the skill to practical situations that may occur at home, in school, or in familiar community settings. This helps make learning meaningful and useful in everyday life.

The teacher serves as a facilitator who provides clear instructions, appropriate support, repetition, feedback, and opportunities for practice. The learner takes an active role by observing, thinking, remembering, making choices, responding, and applying the skill. The level of support can be adjusted according to the learner's needs.

The strategy is implemented throughout the nine functional-skill lesson areas of COGNI-LEARN. Although the activities differ, each lesson follows the same general cognitive process so that learners can gradually move from understanding information to using the skill independently.

Assessment is integrated into the strategy through learner responses, attempts, correct answers, completed activities, progress records, and reflection. These features allow the teacher to monitor learning and identify areas that may require additional practice or support.

Overall, the COGNI-LEARN Cognitive Learning Cycle Strategy uses interactive and functional activities to help learners process information, build understanding, strengthen memory, and transfer learning to everyday situations.
